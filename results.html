Three-way reconciliation: matching the books, the payments and the bank each day: Full results

Paper checks flagged before they cleared fell from 46% at a 5-day window to 9.9% at 15, on one county's public records.

Every figure for each rule, by window and by payment type.

County: clean feeds

Two measures below were added after the results, in the second version. One counts money once per payment. The other gives break ages that count the breaks still open, a Kaplan-Meier estimate.

  • Exact reference. Wrong pairings: 0. Open at an average close, summed over both sides: US$322,953,150.65. Counted once per payment: US$161,049,892.41, 26.60% of the money paid. Breaks: 46,180, of which 3,817 closed within the data and 42,363 were still open at the last close. With the open ones counted, the median age was not reached, because more than half were still open when the data ended.
  • Reference, then amount and date. Wrong pairings: 543, worth US$2,837,842.58. Open at an average close, summed over both sides: US$474,025,021.27. Counted once per payment: US$195,177,513.99, 32.24% of the money paid. Breaks: 51,082, of which 116 closed within the data and 50,966 were still open at the last close. With the open ones counted, the median age was not reached, because more than half were still open when the data ended.
  • Whole payment. Wrong pairings: 0. Open at an average close, summed over both sides: US$4,378,524.91. Counted once per payment: US$4,378,524.91, 0.72% of the money paid. Breaks: 3,953, of which 3,724 closed within the data and 229 were still open at the last close. With the open ones counted, the median age is 4 business days, and 1 in 10 stayed open 34 or more.

Exact reference and whole payment cannot pair wrongly on these feeds, because the truth of which records belong together is the check number itself. Their zeros follow from the data's construction.

Whole-payment matching left US$4.38 million open on average. Of that, US$3.16 million was timing: its counterpart arrived later. US$1.22 million had no counterpart at all, mostly checks never presented. None was open because the rule could not pair records that belong together.

Under the two one-to-one rules, most open money, summed over both sides, had its partner already there. That was US$319 million of US$323 million for exact references and US$470 million of US$474 million for the fallback rule. Neither can pair the invoice lines of a payment that pays several invoices, US$381.5 million of the US$605.4 million paid. The fallback rule also refuses a check that clears after its 5-day window.

Whole-payment matching left the least money open at every one of the 12 month ends.

County: the window for paper checks

Electronic payments never became a break before clearing, at any window. Paper checks did: 46.0% at 5 business days, 17.9% at 10, 9.9% at 15 and 0.78% at 60. A longer window delays the flag on a check that never clears by the same amount.

County: planted events, 20 of each

  • The bank pays one check twice. Correct call: flag. Right calls of 20: exact reference 20, amount-and-date fallback 18, whole payment 20.
  • The bank keys a check number wrong. Correct call: explain. Right calls of 20: exact reference 0, amount-and-date fallback 6, whole payment 0.
  • The bank pays a different amount. Correct call: flag. Right calls of 20: exact reference 20, amount-and-date fallback 20, whole payment 20.
  • One invoice is paid twice, in two payments. Correct call: flag. Right calls of 20: exact reference 0, amount-and-date fallback 1, whole payment 0.
  • A voided check is paid anyway. Correct call: flag. Right calls of 20: exact reference 20, amount-and-date fallback 18, whole payment 20.
  • The bank pays a check the books never issued. Correct call: flag. Right calls of 20: exact reference 20, amount-and-date fallback 18, whole payment 20.
  • An electronic payment clears 8 business days after issue. Correct call: explain. Right calls of 20: exact reference 0, amount-and-date fallback 1, whole payment 0.
  • A payment never clears. Correct call: flag. Right calls of 20: exact reference 20, amount-and-date fallback 19, whole payment 20.

Right calls in all: exact reference 100, amount-and-date fallback 101 and whole payment 100 of 160. The fallback's lead comes from 6 of 20 mistyped check numbers it paired to the right payment. It paired 6 others to a wrong one. Through wrong pairings it also hid 7 real problems in four kinds. They were 2 double-paid checks, 2 voided checks paid, 2 checks never issued and 1 payment that never cleared.

Under the two one-to-one rules every fee row stays open, because the books have no record for a fee: 18,666 breaks against 86. That is a design choice of this study, so the batch rule's edge here follows from it. Whole-batch matching closes a fee row when its batch pairs. The processor paid out US$1,464,064.53 and kept US$51,412.86 in fees.

Version 3: match only a lone fit

Added after the results. With each check number hidden as before, a debit is matched only when exactly one open payment fits; two or more send it to a person. A lone fit cannot be wrong when the true payment is in reach, because the true payment always fits. So every wrong match is a debit whose payment cleared later than the look-back, and that met one look-alike. All of them were paper checks.

Wrong matches fell from 28.3 per 1,000 debits at 5 days to 0.4 at 60, the only look-back under 1 per 1,000. More debits had look-alikes, 13.2% at 5 days and 22.4% at 60. But far fewer found no payment at all, so the share needing a person fell from 44.6% to 22.7%.

Limits

One county's year and one synthetic processor, each run once, so no figure carries a spread. The bank lines carry no payment type, so a debit could fit a payment of the other type. With type codes, the oldest pick's wrong rates could only fall.
