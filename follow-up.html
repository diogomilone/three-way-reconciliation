Three-way reconciliation: matching the books, the payments and the bank each day: Follow-up

An independent author's 22 cases were too few to separate the rules: whole payment made 10 of 10 right calls on the county, the others 9.

Test cases from an author who never saw the rules.

What was done

An independent author who never saw the rules, the planted events or any result wrote 24 events, 12 for the county and 12 for the card processor. The author had a description of the feeds, 20 sample rows of each and a small event grammar, and gave each event its correct call. The brief was fixed before it ran, and the rules did not change.

The result

22 of 24 events applied. Right calls on the county: whole payment 10 of 10, exact reference 9, amount-and-date fallback 9. On the processor: whole payment 11 of 12, exact reference 10, amount-and-date fallback 9. 17 of the 22 were called right by all three rules, so the comparison rests on the other 5, too few to separate the rules. The design keeps the two data sets apart, so no total across them is given.

Its one miss was a processing fee charged twice, hidden by netting, the same blind spot as the planted fee overcharge. The one-to-one rules flagged a supplier credit netted into one check, a fee taken in the next batch and a partial refund, all ordinary. The fallback also paired a payout whose remittance named no real batch, by amount, without a flag.

  • Bank debits the same check twice (flag): every rule right
  • Paid debit missing from the bank feed (flag): every rule right
  • Bank debit contains a major amount error (flag): every rule right
  • Invoice line imported twice (flag): every rule right
  • Invoice line lost from the books (flag): every rule right
  • Electronic payment total corrupted (flag): every rule right
  • Bank check reference damaged (flag): every rule right
  • Invoice posting arrives the following business day (explain): every rule right
  • Supplier credit netted into one check (explain): wrong: exact reference, amount-and-date fallback
  • Unpresented check formally voided (explain): every rule right
  • Processor omits a booked charge (flag): every rule right
  • Processor settles a Canadian charge twice (flag): every rule right
  • Processor settles the wrong sale amount (flag): every rule right
  • Refund omitted from the company books (flag): every rule right
  • Bank credits only part of a payout (flag): every rule right
  • Bank remittance names a nonexistent batch (flag): wrong: amount-and-date fallback
  • Processing fee charged twice (flag): wrong: whole payment
  • Refund posted to the books one day late (explain): every rule right
  • Payout reaches the bank a day late (explain): every rule right
  • Charge misses the settlement cutoff (explain): every rule right
  • Processing fee deducted in the next batch (explain): wrong: exact reference, amount-and-date fallback
  • Customer receives a partial refund after settlement (explain): wrong: exact reference, amount-and-date fallback

Two events not applied

A check held longer by its supplier and an electronic payment one day late put a number range on the check number, which the grammar reads as text. Both were applied afterwards with the number read as a number, as a labelled side result. Every rule flagged the held check, the wrong call. Only whole-payment matching let the one-day delay pass.

Limits

One author and 22 events, too few to separate the rules.
