Three-way reconciliation: matching the books, the payments and the bank each day: Glossary

The 22 terms this study uses, from books to void, each in one plain sentence.

Each term in plain words.

Terms

Books
The company's own record of what it owes or is owed, line by line.
Payment run
The list of payments the books send out, each paying one or more invoices.
Settlement report
The card processor's daily file of what it settled, what it charged in fees and what it paid out.
Batch
One day's settled items at the processor, paid out together as one transfer.
Payout
The single transfer from the processor to the bank for one batch, net of fees and refunds.
Bank line
One debit or credit on the bank statement.
Reference
The shared identifier that links two records: a check number, a payment document or a batch number.
Pairing
Two records a rule has matched as the same money.
Wrong pairing
A pairing of two records that do not belong together.
Close
The check run at the end of a business day over every record seen so far.
Window
How many business days a record may stay unpaired before it counts as a problem.
Look-back
How many business days before a bank line amount-and-date matching searches for its payment.
Lone fit
The only open payment within US$1.00 of a bank line, issued within the look-back.
Look-alike
A bank line that two or more open payments fit, so a person has to choose.
In transit
Unpaired, but still inside its window.
Break
Unpaired after its window: an item someone must look at.
Netting
Adding credits, refunds and fees into one total, so only the total has to match.
Fallback
A second way to pair a bank line when its reference finds no partner; here, amount and date.
Hidden-reference test
Each bank line's reference hidden in turn, to see whether amount and date alone find the right payment.
Kaplan-Meier estimate
A way to read how long breaks stay open that also counts the breaks still open at the end of the data.
Chargeback
A customer dispute that takes the money of a charge back.
Void
A payment cancelled before the bank paid it.

Limits

Definitions as used on these pages, which may be narrower than an accounting standard's.
